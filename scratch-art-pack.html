Decision first

The candidate faces: abstract or portraits?

During the search beat, the hero flashes twelve searched candidates. Today they are abstract person-silhouette glyphs on green and bronze gradients. Replacing them with generated photoreal faces would imply that twelve specific real experts exist, and it brushes the locked "no real team faces in the hero" rule. This is the one call worth making on purpose. Here are both paths.

Recommended

A · Keep people abstract

Candidates stay silhouettes. The matched expert becomes a stylized, non-photoreal avatar. You generate only atmosphere plus the social image.

  • Honest: nothing implies a named real person is on the roster
  • Stays inside the "no real faces in the hero" rule cleanly
  • The green-is-people, bronze-is-expert language keeps carrying meaning
  • Fewer assets to generate, review, and keep on brand

Generate: OG, film grain, globe atmosphere, matched-expert avatar.

Alternative

B · Generic portrait art

Candidates and the matched expert become diverse generated portraits, framed explicitly as representative, not real individuals.

  • More cinematic, more human warmth in the search beat
  • Needs a visible "representative, not real people" cue near the film
  • Risk: a viewer reads them as your actual roster
  • More art to generate and keep diverse without drifting stocky

Only if you accept the "not real people" framing on the page.

My recommendation

Take Option A. The film's whole honesty is that the human is real and the moment is real. Photoreal strangers in the search beat trade that away for polish you do not need, since the matched card is where the emotional weight lands, not the flickering candidates. Keep candidates abstract, and make the one expert who joins feel crafted rather than stock. The Option B prompt is included at the end if you decide the warmth is worth the framing cost.
