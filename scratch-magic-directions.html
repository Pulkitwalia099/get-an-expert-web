The magic section: now you try.

The centerpiece that comes right after the hero film. Three concepts to react to, with tradeoffs and a recommendation. This is a taste gate, not a build. Nothing here touches the site.

For: Pulkit Sits: below the hero film, on the marketing page Job: show breadth, keep the story going Status: react and pick a direction

The frame

The hero told one story. This beat proves it happens everywhere.

The film "The Wall" is a single, cinematic thread: you hit a wall inside your session, a globe searches, one expert emerges, joins with your full context, delivers in an hour, and you never left your seat. Beautiful, but it is one ask and one craft. The magic section is where the site widens out. The product truth is bigger than rescue: to build anything great, you need real humans to bring the vision alive. You delegate whole functions. You hire the specialist you could never reach. The job of this section is to make that breadth felt, as a natural next beat, not a second screening of the same film.

Why it fits after the hero

The film ends on "you never left your session," a passive close. This is the active answer: now you try, ask for anything. It reuses the exact composer and the exact "search resolves to one human" mechanic, so it feels like the same world, but every ask lands in a different domain, which is how breadth gets proven without a second film.

Effort, risk, fit

Effort · Medium-low Risk · Low Builds on hero · Medium Shows breadth · High

Six to nine real outcomes with a human name, a one-line track record, and a portrait each. This is the most content-hungry option and the most honest one.

It takes the film's strongest image, the expert joining your session, and generalizes it. The reframe is the payoff: the wall in the film was a story device, and here the same join is shown as universal.

Effort, risk, fit

Effort · Medium-high Risk · Medium-high Builds on hero · High Shows breadth · Medium

Main risk: it sits closest to the hero's own split-screen visual and could feel like the film on repeat. Tabbed static states, not a scroll film, keep it distinct. It also needs three believable non-coding surfaces built, which is the heaviest lift here.

What it needs from Pulkit

Sign-off on the three surfaces to show, and one short in-session script per surface. More engineering than A or B for the mock scenes.

Recommendation

Lead with A. Fold B in beneath it as proof. Hold C for later.

Concept A, "Ask anything," is the truest next beat. The hero closes passively with "you never left your session." A opens actively with "now you try, ask for anything." It reuses the exact composer and the exact search-to-one-human mechanic the visitor just watched, so the world stays coherent, and because every ask lands in a different domain, it proves breadth through the visitor's own hands rather than through a second film.

  1. A is the centerpiece. The live composer, cycling real asks, resolving to a curated expert and a real outcome.
  2. B rides directly beneath it as a quiet proof strip. Try an ask above, then see it already delivered across domains below. A gives agency, B gives evidence, and together they cover both.
  3. C is the v2.1 swing. The reframe is strong, but it is the heaviest build and sits closest to the film, so it is better once there is room to do it justice.

Fastest honest path to shipping: A can go live with copy alone, since the curated triples above are the entire content requirement. B can grow tile by tile as real deliveries land.
